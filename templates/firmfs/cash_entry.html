Names the medical aid society or other payee. The payment only clears the payroll payable (Dr payable, Cr cash): it creates no bill and no expense, and the supplier's account is not affected.

Paying staff and what the payroll owes: pick one of the four Payroll choices - Salaries paid for net pay (clears Net salaries payable), PAYE and other taxes paid for ZIMRA, NSSA contributions paid for NSSA, and Medical aid and other deductions paid for the medical aid society (or any other deduction taken off pay; clears Other payroll deductions payable). Choose the payee under Paid to (supplier) (for example FBC Medical Aid): it is named on the entry and on the supplier's page, but the payment is still Dr the payroll payable / Cr cash. It is not a bill, not a supplier balance and not an expense; do not file medical aid paid over under a medical aid expense category. The expense itself comes from the finalized staff payroll, so do not also file these under a Salaries expense category.

Balance sheet accounts (assets, liabilities, equity): choose one when the money is not an expense, for example repaying a loan, paying a deposit or a prepayment, or money from or to an owner. Money out debits the account and credits the bank or cash account; money in does the reverse. It goes straight to the statements and the ledger, so do not also journal it or type it on the year-end page. Balance sheet accounts can be charged from USD cash book accounts only; to add accounts to this list, add them in the chart of accounts (Firm Finance > Ledger).

A payment filed under an expense category (Fuel, Rent ...) is recorded in the Expense register as paid, so it appears in the income statement and cannot be entered twice. Any other entry here changes the bank or cash balance only. If it also affects the income statement or balance sheet (a loan, a dividend, share capital, bank charges), record that on the year-end page too; the year-end page lists these cash book movements to help.
