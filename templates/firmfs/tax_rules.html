What the Income Tax Act says about these allowances

  • Buildings ({{ act_ref('sch4.6', text='Fourth Schedule, para 6') }}): a commercial building gets 2.5% of cost a year from the year it is first used. Farm improvements, industrial buildings, railway lines, staff housing and tobacco barns get 5% of cost, or 25% of cost from the year after first use if a special initial allowance was claimed. Land gets nothing.
  • Machinery, equipment, furniture, computers, vehicles ({{ act_ref('sch4.7', text='para 7') }}): wear and tear is "such sum as the Commissioner thinks reasonable" unless a special initial allowance was claimed, when it is 25% of cost from the year after first use. The 10%, 20% and 25% starter rates here are practice, not figures written in the Act. No wear and tear is allowed in the year an SIA is claimed ({{ act_ref('sch4.3', text='para 3(2)') }}).
  • Special initial allowance ({{ act_ref('sch4.9', text='para 9') }}): the rate depends on the year of assessment, and has changed several times - 50% for years from 1 January 2001; for years from 1 January 2014, 25% with 50% in the first year of claiming and 25% in each of the next two years; 100% for a licensed investor from 1 January 2017; small and medium enterprises on their own terms. Read the paragraph and set the rate that applies to the year-end you are working on.
  • Passenger motor vehicles ({{ act_ref('sch4.14', text='para 14') }}): for vehicles bought from 1 January 2009 the cost above US$10,000 is disregarded. Enter that as the vehicle class's cost cap.
  • Scrapping and sale ({{ act_ref('sch4.4', text='para 4') }}): a scrapped asset gets cost less allowances already made and any amount received; the full text of all of these is in the Income Tax Act.
